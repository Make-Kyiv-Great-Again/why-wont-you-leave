# The Voice

The Voice should never sound openly evil.

It is Evan's fear and guilt trying to keep him inside the apartment.

It uses three main strategies:

### 1. Denial

> Don't remember.

> You don't need to know.

> There was nothing you could have changed.

### 2. Guilt

> You made her cry a lot.

> You should have noticed.

> And now she's gone.

### 3. Temptation

> You can play for her again.

> You can go back there.

> You can be happy again.

> Just don't leave.

And finally:

> You promised we'd spend our lives together.

The Voice isn't a ghost.

It is the part of Evan that would rather live inside his memories than accept that Grace is gone.

---

# The Moral Question

The central moral question of the game is:

> **Do we have the right to hold on to someone simply because we're afraid of losing them?**

A deeper version:

> **If loving someone means respecting their freedom, can we still call it love when we refuse to let them go?**

And the question that belongs specifically to Evan:

> **Is it better to preserve someone in your memory exactly as you want them to be, or to accept their loss and continue living without them?**

The four endings then represent four different choices:

1. **Wrong artifacts + Stay** — Fear wins. Evan dies.
2. **Wrong artifacts + Leave** — Evan chooses life, but without understanding the truth. He wakes up and cries.
3. **Right artifacts + Stay** — Evan understands the truth but chooses guilt over life. He dies.
4. **Right artifacts + Leave** — Evan understands the truth, accepts Grace's forgiveness, and chooses to live.

The important point is that **the correct artifacts don't save Evan automatically**.

They only give him enough truth to make a conscious choice.

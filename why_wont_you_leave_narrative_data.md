# WHY WON'T YOU LEAVE? — Narrative Data Specification

## Acts

The game has five acts:

- **Act I — The Apartment**
- **Act II — The Things She Hid**
- **Act III — The Choice**
- **Act IV — The Crash**
- **Act V — The Door**

The opening sequence before Act I is **not an act**. It is the black-screen accident sequence.

---

# Room Layouts

The apartment has exactly four rooms. Their geometry and major furniture never change between acts. Only lighting, post-processing, object states, and additional small details may change.

## Bedroom

The bedroom must always contain:

- Bed
- Bedside table
- Window
- Wardrobe
- Desk
- Chair
- Small bookshelf
- Bedroom door leading to hallway
- Floor space underneath the bed
- Wall/decoration space for photographs
- Desk surface for the phone and later hospital bracelet

Important artifact positions:

- Photograph of Evan and Grace — bedside table
- Travel bag — wardrobe
- Phone — desk
- Acceptance letter — under travel bag
- Unsent letter — underneath mattress
- Hotel reservation — phone
- Hospital bracelet — desk
- Windshield fragment — underneath bed

The bedroom should feel like the most personal room in the apartment.

---

## Hallway

The hallway must always contain:

- Exit door
- Bedroom door
- Bathroom door
- Kitchen entrance
- Small cabinet beside the exit
- Coat rack
- One or two coats
- Wall mirror
- Small shelf / decorative surface

Important artifact positions:

- Car keys — cabinet beside exit
- Photograph of Grace with another man — inside Grace's coat pocket

The hallway should visually emphasize the exit door throughout the game.

---

## Bathroom

The bathroom must always contain:

- Sink
- Mirror
- Medicine cabinet
- Bathtub or shower
- Toilet
- Small storage shelf
- Bathroom door leading to hallway

Important artifact positions:

- Grace's medicine — medicine cabinet
- Old anniversary note — behind/inside the mirror

The bathroom has very few important interactions. It should feel slightly detached from the main story, which helps the false artifacts feel believable.

---

## Kitchen

The kitchen must always contain:

- Kitchen counter
- Sink
- Refrigerator
- Stove
- Dining table
- Two chairs
- Kitchen drawers
- Trash bin
- Window
- Kitchen entrance from hallway

Important artifact positions:

- Torn acceptance envelope — trash
- Bank receipt — drawer
- Train/plane ticket — drawer
- Spare key — back of drawer

The kitchen should feel like the place where the couple normally dealt with practical aspects of their life.

---

# Artifact Data

Each artifact has three stages:

1. **Interaction dialogue** — Evan examines the object.
2. **Remember / Forget choice** — player decides whether to retain the memory.
3. **Memory dialogue** — black screen; no answer options.

The Voice dialogue happens after selected memories and is separate from the memory itself.

# ACT I — EXIT

After the player has interacted with the required Act I objects, the exit door becomes available.

Evan approaches it.

The hallway distorts.

The camera shakes.

The sound becomes muffled.

The Voice:

> "Why won't you leave?"

Evan:

> "Who are you?"

Voice:

> "You know."

Evan:

> "No."

Voice:

> "Not yet."

Cut to black.

# ACT V — THE DOOR

There are no artifacts.

There is no apartment.

There is only blackness.

A single white door stands in the center.

Evan approaches it.

Text appears:

> **LEAVE?**

Options:

> **YES**

> **NO**

The player's choice determines the ending.

---

# Important Implementation Rule

The artifact system should **not require the player to remember every TRUE artifact to understand the story**.

The first playthrough should be intentionally imperfect.

A player might remember:

- Grace's photograph
- the travel bag
- the photograph of Grace with another man
- the hotel reservation
- the acceptance letter

and conclude:

> "Grace was leaving Evan."

On a second playthrough, they understand that the misleading memories are still real but **do not belong to the causal chain of the accident**.

The ideal reconstruction is:

> **Acceptance → Grace's secret plan → financial sacrifice → ticket → Evan discovers it → argument → Grace tries to stop him driving → crash → Grace dies → Evan survives → guilt creates the Voice.**

That gives the Remember / Forget mechanic an actual gameplay purpose: **the player isn't deciding which memories are factually true. They are deciding which memories Evan is willing to carry with him.**
